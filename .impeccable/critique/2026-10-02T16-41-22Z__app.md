## Design Specificity Verdict

The product has recognizable adaptations: the 鹿鸣 deer identity, six themes, configurable navigation, and timeline-specific media treatment. The daily visual language still relies on Elk's familiar timeline structure, so most identity comes from branding and theme rather than from a distinct interaction system.

The CLI scan of `app/` returned four warnings:

- Two bounce-easing warnings point to the bottom-nav indicator and media press feedback. These are intentional spring-like interactions in this project.
- The broken-image warning points to a runtime `src` created by `getEmojiAttributes`; the detector cannot resolve the dynamic value, so this is a false positive for valid emoji reactions.
- The side-tab warning matches a left border on semantic blockquotes, not a card accent. This is a false positive.

No confirmed detector finding requires removing a design pattern. The browser interface did not expose script injection, so no reliable user-visible overlay or console detector report is available.

## Overall Impression

The interface is a coherent, adaptable social client. Browsing reads cleanly; writing a post and configuring preferences demand the most attention. The biggest opportunity is to make advanced actions discoverable without presenting every option at once.

## What's Working

- The left navigation, centered timeline, and right search rail provide a stable layout. Profile, notifications, and feed pages keep a consistent reading width.
- Status cards maintain a clear order for author, post, media, and interaction actions.
- Theme, font-size, and bottom-navigation preferences let users tailor the client beyond palette alone.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] The compose toolbar is visually dense.** Media, polls, scheduling, language, content warning, visibility, quote controls, and other actions share a compact icon row. Most have tooltips and accessible names, but users must hover or trial-and-error to understand the icons; touch users cannot hover. **Fix:** keep frequent actions visible and group advanced post settings under a labeled control, with the current choices summarized. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
2. **[P2] Preference surfaces require repeated scanning.** The preferences page has more than twenty switches across several sections. The appearance page shows fifteen bottom-nav choices at once, while allowing five selections and requiring More. **Fix:** collapse experimental or low-frequency controls and show selected/available counts; group navigation choices by purpose. Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`.
3. **[P2] Chinese localization falls back to English in visible controls.** The preview shows “Scheduled posts” and “Collections”; preferences also show “Video sound on by default” and “Hide quote count”. The corresponding keys are absent from `locales/zh-CN.json`. **Fix:** fill the Chinese translations and check each settings page for fallback strings. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-time user:** the compose toolbar relies on icon recognition and hover help; settings labels that unexpectedly switch to English add uncertainty.
- **Alex, power user:** secondary destinations such as conversations, bookmarks, lists, scheduled posts, and settings require expanding More. Letting users pin frequently used destinations could reduce repeat navigation.

## Minor Observations

- The preferences page already groups options into social and experimental sections; collapsing the experimental section by default could shorten the initial scan.
- The signed-in mock profile redirects `/` to `/home`; the true signed-out entry could not be viewed live. Its sign-in layout was checked from source only.
- Browser inspection covered the primary desktop routes: home feed, explore, profile, notifications, settings, compose, and a status detail attempt. Mobile behavior was checked in responsive source, not a narrow live viewport; not every leaf route was opened.
- Right-rail space on the feed is mostly reserved for search. Keep this quiet unless useful contextual content is available; filling it without purpose would add noise.

## Questions to Consider

- Could the compose surface show the actions people use most and reveal advanced settings on demand?
- Can the five-slot navigation rule be clear at a glance while still making More mandatory?
- Which Chinese locale should be treated as complete before settings are considered polished?
